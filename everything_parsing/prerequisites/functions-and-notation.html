Functions & the Notation Wall

Parsing papers and textbooks lean on maybe a dozen symbols. None are hard; the barrier is only that nobody lists them all in one place. Here they all are.

01

Functions: total vs. partial

Function f : A → B — a rule mapping each input in A to at most one output in B. A is the domain, B the codomain.

Total: defined for every element of A. A DFA transition δ : Q × Σ → Q is total — every (state, symbol) pair has a target.

Partial: defined for some elements of A. An NFA transition δ : Q × (Σ ∪ {ε}) → 𝒫(Q) can map a pair to ∅ — "no move." Parser lookup tables are usually partial: a blank cell means "syntax error."

Why it matters: "the table has a hole" and "the table maps to the empty set" are different failure modes, and LL/LR construction depends on telling them apart.

03

Reading a production out loud

  E  →  E + T  |  T

  "An E is either  an E, then a plus token, then a T
                   or  just a T."

Capital E, T: nonterminals (names of shapes). +: a terminal (a real token). |: "or". The rule is recursive — E appears on its own right-hand side — which is exactly the property Module 16 has to remove before a top-down parser can use it.

Two meanings of one bar

| inside a grammar means "alternative." |w| with a symbol between two bars means "length of w". Context tells them apart, but the collision trips people up the first few times.
